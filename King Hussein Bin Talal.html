King Hussein Bin Talal: A Legacy of Leadership

Why was his Majesty known as The Humane King?

Written By: Fadi Samarneh

Published On: March 1st 2026

His Majesty King Hussein bin Talal is one of the most significant figures in Jordanian history. Often referred to as the father of modern Jordan, he is remembered as the leader who guided his country through struggle to become a center of peace and stability in the Middle East. His legacy is cherished among all Jordanians; he is also widely known as Malik Al-Insan (“The Humane King”).

Figure 01: An Image of King Hussein bin Talal Giving a Press Conference

King Hussein Bin Talal was born on the 14th of November 1935, to Prince Talal bin Abdullah and Princess Zein al-Sharaf bint Jamil. He studied in Amman until elementary school, after which he attended Victoria College in Alexandria, Egypt. He later studied at Harrow School in England. He received his military education at the Royal Military Academy Sandhurst in England.

The Development of Jordan

His long reign saw Jordan develop to an unprecedented extent, with his early years focused on improving the quality of life for Jordanians. He did so by investing heavily in Jordan's main factories and industries, and hence building a network of highways throughout the kingdom.

Figure 02: King Hussein bin Talal Shaking Hands with former Israeli Prime Minister Yitzhak Rabin in the presence of former U.S. President Bill Clinton

In 1950, only 10% of Jordanians had water, sanitation, and electricity. By 1960, that number grew to 33%, and by 1996, 85.5% of Jordanians had access to these necessities.

Attempts at Promoting Peace

King Hussien did have many struggles at promoting peace throughout his period of reign. One of the key developments he made was drafting the UNSC Resolution after the Arab-Israeli war of 1967, which called for the withdrawal of all Israeli soldiers from occupied land in the 1967 war in exchange for peace.

Figure 03: His Majesty King Hussein with Saddam Hussein

King Hussein had a great commitment to democracy and human rights. He helped transform Jordan into a model of human rights and peace in the region, as it has by far the cleanest human rights record in the region. In 1990, His Majesty drafted a national charter that serves as a guideline for democratic institutionalization and political pluralism in the country.

In 1978, His Majesty King Hussein married Queen Noor. They had two sons, Hamzah and Hashem as well as two daughters, Iman and Raiyah. He also had three other sons, the current King of Jordan, King Abdullah, Faisel, and Ali, as well as 5 daughters, Alia, Zein, Aisha, Haya, and Abeer from three previous marriages.

Figure 04: His Majesty King Hussein bin Talal Memorial

Even after being diagnosed with lymph gland cancer in July of 1998, King Hussein bin Talal was able to convince Netanyahu and Arafat to agree on overcoming their challenges, gaining the credit and respect of Bill Clinton. At 11:50 AM on February 7th, 1999, King Hussein bin Talal passed away, with his funeral hosting the greatest gathering of foreign leaders since 1995.
